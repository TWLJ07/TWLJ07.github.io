Autonomous Marine Monitoring Buoy

This project is a conceptual mechanical design of an autonomous marine buoy developed to monitor localized marine environments for fish and lobster activity. The system integrates acoustic sensing, onboard data storage, wireless communication, and a sealed pressure-resistant housing into a compact, field-deployable platform. The design emphasizes reliability in harsh marine environments through robust sealing, modular internal components, and maintainable assemblies while supporting long-term underwater data collection.

Pressure Relief Valve

  • Integrated pressure relief valve for internal pressure management.
  • Cracking pressure: 15 psi.
  • Vent rate: 5 L/min.
  • Protects internal electronics from pressure buildup while maintaining enclosure integrity.

Hydrophone

  • High-frequency underwater acoustic sensor for marine monitoring.
  • Frequency range: 10 Hz – 200 kHz.
  • Rated for operation at depths of up to 100 m.
  • Enables acoustic detection and monitoring of marine life and underwater activity.

Battery System

  • High-capacity 96-cell battery pack providing long-duration operation.
  • Mounted securely within the pressure housing for optimal weight distribution and protection.

Electronics Chassis

  • Modular internal electronics assembly containing eight 64 GB SD cards for onboard data storage.
  • WiFi PCB for wireless communication and data retrieval.
  • General/Power PCB for power distribution and system connectivity.
  • Designed for organized cable routing and straightforward maintenance.

Pressure Housing

  • Cylindrical waterproof housing that protects the battery pack and electronics chassis.
  • Sealed using precision-machined endcaps and O-ring interfaces to prevent water ingress.
  • Integrated handling rings simplify deployment and recovery operations.
  • Endcaps provide mounting locations for the PRV, hydrophone, McCartney power connector, and anode.

Buoy Assembly

  • Floating buoy structure designed to support the underwater housing while maintaining stable surface positioning.
  • Protective guard surrounding the hydrophone to reduce the risk of impact damage during deployment and retrieval.
  • Surface-mounted navigation light for improved visibility.
  • External WiFi antenna positioned above the waterline to enable wireless communication when the buoy is surfaced.
